Fruitopia

June 2026 Farhaan Beeharry
mobile app flutter marketplace supabase ecommerce
Fruitopia is a two-sided farm-to-table marketplace built with Flutter — one binary, two entirely different apps. Customers browse fresh produce, add to cart and track their delivery; vendors get a seller workspace to manage inventory, fulfil orders, plan delivery routes and read sales analytics. Which app you get is decided by your account, and each side has its own navigation shell.

For customers, Fruitopia is a focused storefront: a home feed of fresh picks, category browsing, product pages with ratings, stock status and unit / quantity selection, a favourites list, a cart with a live order summary and delivery fee, and order tracking that moves from pending to confirmed to delivered.

The delivery model is the part most marketplaces get wrong, and it is modelled properly here. The seller defines delivery regions, each one either office or home. An office region contains a list of buildings, so a customer ordering to work simply picks their building from a list instead of typing an address that a driver then has to interpret; a home region falls back to a typed street address. Only active regions are offered, and every region name is bilingual.

For vendors, the same app flips into a seller workspace. A dashboard surfaces daily revenue, order counts and stock levels; inventory screens create and update products and publish a daily list of what is actually available today; each incoming order shows the customer's delivery address or building; and dedicated views handle delivery-route planning, seasonal pre-orders, sales analytics and broadcast notifications back to customers.

The codebase reflects production discipline: a clean domain / data / presentation split per feature, each with its own dependency-injection module; flutter_bloc for state and get_it for injection; go_router shells for the two navigation trees; and a mock-data layer that swaps to a live Supabase backend without touching UI code. It is fully localised in English and French with no hardcoded strings, on a Material 3 design system with content-shaped skeleton loaders rather than spinners.

  • Pre-order management
  • Sales analytics
  • Broadcast notifications to customers
  • Delivery region and building administration, office or home per region
  • Category management
